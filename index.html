01 / Executive thesis

The issue is coexistence, not a broken bank.

DBS has already built a sophisticated digital and AI-enabled operating model. The next challenge is governing how legacy cores, distributed services and regional platforms evolve together.

The proposed strategy is to modernise business capabilities in controlled increments while keeping authoritative ledgers stable. It makes application and workload decisions from business criticality, dependency evidence, change velocity and recovery tolerance - not from a blanket preference for cloud or microservices.

The strategic tension: moving from monolithic mainframes to distributed services can increase the number of dependencies, network hops and failure paths before it reduces structural risk. Service decoupling, runtime observability, automated reconciliation and change governance must be built into the migration itself.

What is working

DBS has established a Board Technology Committee, Tech Future Town and the R.I.S.E. framework; it also reports mature digital engineering, AI industrialisation and a multi-year mainframe transformation programme.

What still needs validation

Internal application counts, runtime dependency maps, MIPS and licensing baselines, hidden regional duplication, actual production latency and transformation budgets are not public. These are Wave 0 discovery items, not facts to assume.

03 / Portfolio discipline

Know the estate before changing it.

The first management problem is incomplete portfolio and dependency visibility. The proposed 20-attribute register becomes the baseline for all later decisions.

Business capabilities to protect first

Domestic and cross-border payments; core deposits and account servicing; wealth management; enterprise data and AI; critical risk and resilience services.

Portfolio register should join four views

Business ownership and criticality, application lifecycle and technical health, runtime dependencies, and operating / financial cost - with attestation by business and technology owners.

Seven-gate application disposition logic

The six dispositions are supported by seven decision gates. Decisions require documented rationale, risk review and audit trail; replacement is not the default end-state.

01 / Retain

Keep what is sound

Preserve business-critical applications with acceptable cost, resilience and lifecycle posture.

02 / Retire

Remove duplication

Decommission redundant applications only after usage, records and dependencies are verified.

03 / Rehost

Move the runtime

Shift workloads with minimal code change where infrastructure exit is the primary driver.

04 / Replatform

Upgrade the platform

Change database, runtime or middleware while preserving core business logic.

05 / Refactor

Reshape the capability

Incrementally modularise high-change domains through a Strangler Fig approach.

06 / Replace

Buy or rebuild selectively

Proceed only where economics, control, regulatory fit and exitability justify it.

04 / Mainframe strategy

Three speeds. No big bang.

The report rejects an all-at-once core replacement. Workloads should be classified by coupling intensity, batch vs. real-time behaviour, business differentiation and failure consequence.

SPEED 01 / NEAR TERM

Retain & optimise

Keep authoritative ledger posting stable. Offload high-frequency balance and enquiry reads to CDC-fed caches, tune batch windows and remove avoidable MIPS spikes.

SPEED 02 / TARGETED

Replatform

Move end-of-support runtimes, middleware and bounded batch workloads to supported infrastructure while preserving proven business logic.

SPEED 03 / SELECTIVE

Strangler Fig refactor

Incrementally extract high-change payment and enquiry logic into domain services, leaving the authoritative ledger in place until reconciliation and failover are proven.

Mainframe workload decision matrix

Illustrative decision logic from the report: exact placement requires workload profiling, application dependency validation, MIPS telemetry and business-owner confirmation.

Sequence matters: read-query offload first to relieve peak MIPS pressure; targeted replatforming next for unsupported infrastructure; selective refactoring for domains where agility and differentiation justify the added coexistence complexity.

06 / Resilience by design

Migration is a controlled risk event.

The DBS Predict, Prevent, Detect, Recover framework should operate through every migration gate, not as a compliance checklist after delivery.

01 / Predict

See the blast radius

Runtime dependency graphs, capacity forecasts under 3x peak load, failure-mode analysis and critical-journey mapping.

02 / Prevent

Make unsafe change hard

Immutable infrastructure as code, automated policy gates, near-production test environments, contract testing and independent QA.

03 / Detect

Observe the customer journey

Distributed traces, synthetic probes every 60 seconds, real-time reconciliation alerts and centralised incident command.

04 / Recover

Prove the escape route

Rehearsed sub-15-minute rollback for Strangler facades, realistic DR flips, alternate channels and independent recovery zones.

09 / Execution roadmap

90 days to evidence. Then four gated waves.

No multi-year capital commitment should be locked before internal application, dependency, MIPS, cloud and risk baselines have been validated.

Days 1–30Understand and assess: interviews, CMDB/APM traces, MIPS telemetry, 20-attribute inventory and risk baseline.
Days 31–60Design and prioritise: 6R workshops, workload classification, target architecture, ADRs, financial model and BRD/RTM.
Days 61–90Validate and present: regional stakeholder validation, UAT and rollback plan, stress-test assumptions, secure BTC go-ahead.
Wave 0 · Months 1–6 after discovery

Foundation & governance

Complete application and runtime dependency inventory; establish ARB/ADR governance; ratify API, event, Zero Trust and IaC standards; build cloud landing zones and three-way reconciliation engine; profile MIPS/workloads.

Gate G0: full Tier-1/2 owner attestation, no unmapped critical dependencies, security and DR readiness.
Wave 1 · Months 6–15

Low-risk modernisation

Retire initial duplicate regional applications; offload read-only balance enquiries through CDC caches; standardise API gateway and legacy adapters; rehost/replatform Tier-2 EOSL applications; extend observability.

Gate G1: 3x peak load tests, zero-variance reconciliation, clean low-risk retirement and no Sev-1 incidents.
Wave 2 · Months 12–30

Critical core & mainframe

Incrementally modularise domestic/cross-border payment slices; replatform selected transaction and batch workloads; run 3–6 month parallel shadow cycles; execute canary cutovers with rollback assurance.

Gate G2/G3: 100% UAT and pen-test pass, zero-variance across 3 business cycles, DR flip ≤2 hours and CIO/BU/CRO/CFO sign-off.
Wave 3 · Months 24–42

Regional scale & harvest

Reuse validated Singapore patterns in Hong Kong, Taiwan, India and Indonesia within local data-residency boundaries; decommission legacy systems; step down MIPS/vendor contracts; embed FinOps.

Gate G4: 30-day zero-traffic confirmation, archives complete, legacy licences terminated and ≥95% benefit capture verified by CFO.
10 / Leadership agenda

Eight decisions to start with.

The report's ranked recommendations favour evidence-led, reversible steps before critical-core transformation.

Mandate a runtime-verified portfolio baseline.

Reconcile CMDB and declared inventories with APM/network traces across all Tier-1 journeys. Make this the entry condition for major migration.

Adopt the three-speed mainframe strategy.

Offload enquiry traffic, replatform bounded end-of-support workloads and refactor high-change domains incrementally - while protecting authoritative ledgers.

Make three-way financial reconciliation non-negotiable.

Require zero variance across multiple business cycles before cutover and before decommissioning.

Establish an enforceable API / event / Zero Trust golden path.

Standardise idempotency, semantic versioning, circuit breakers, event dead-letter handling, mTLS and least-privilege access.

Embed Predict-Prevent-Detect-Recover into every wave.

Prove rollback, failover, synthetic journey monitoring, observability and independent risk sign-off at each gate.

Use Finance-validated economics to release capital.

Replace illustrative MIPS, cloud and vendor assumptions with extracted baselines; track direct benefits separately from risk-adjusted benefits.

Strengthen executive and architecture decision rights.

Use BTC oversight, CIO-chaired steering, ARB/ADRs, EASRE independent QA and CRO/Line 2 review for high-risk exceptions.

Prove Singapore patterns before regional reuse.

Scale to Hong Kong, Taiwan, India and Indonesia only after proving data residency, operating model and recovery patterns locally.

The management proposition

Change the parts that need to change.
Protect the things customers rely on.

DBS's modernisation opportunity is to reduce legacy cost and complexity without recreating the operational risk that the bank has spent years addressing. A runtime-verified portfolio, workload-specific migration, composable coexistence architecture, hard reconciliation and recovery gates, and CFO-led benefit validation provide a disciplined route from Tech Future Town ambition to measurable enterprise outcomes.

The first approval should be for the 90-Day Discovery Programme - not a blanket core replacement.
